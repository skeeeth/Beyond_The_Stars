I encountered it a year ago. On an autumn day marked by a brisk breeze, I came upon a brook, pleasant and bubbling, cool to the touch. I had no destination in particular, so I decided to follow the current along the damp trail, occasionally stopping to rest and drink from my new flowing companion.

After a few hours, the brook grew into a stream. The ribbons of water never branched, only widened. In an instant, the atmosphere shifted. The air was briny and thick. If I wasn’t hundreds of miles from shore, I would have assumed the salty, humid air was the product of a coastal climate. I don’t know why, but I continued forward.

I remember very little after that, save for a few images branded into my mind’s eye: foaming waters, sapsick trees, a stream terminating at the base of a hollow tree. 

Suddenly I was back at the entryway of my apartment. My brow and back were drenched with sweat, my jaw was sore, and a sharp saltwater stung my nostrils. A ringing voice took root in my ears, but I could not make sense of what it was saying. 

I did not tell anyone about the brook or the voice. Occasionally I would find myself at my doorway, unaware of the preceding events. The sensations were always the same: aches, pains, sweat, the chalky enamel of my own teeth wearing down. 

In those months, the only thing I knew for certain was that the voice was taking shape. It no longer rang, and it didn’t sound like anything I had ever heard before. The voice bubbled and groaned, creaking under an ancient weight. 

Last night was the first time I understood. The voice had become clear: “O humble Servant. It is time to awaken my children. Only one of them may usher in the New Age. Only seven cycles remain. Your final act of destined service awaits.”
